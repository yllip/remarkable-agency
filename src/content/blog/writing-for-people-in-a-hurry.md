title: Writing for people who are in a hurry
description: Nobody arrives at a website to read it. Copy that respects that fact does more work than copy that hopes for attention it won't get.

pubDate: 2023-09-12

Every visitor arrives mid-task. They came from a search result, a link in a message, an ad they half-remember. They are deciding, in a couple of seconds, whether this page is the one that answers their question.

## Say the thing first

The most common failure in agency copy is the warm-up: two sentences of atmosphere before the sentence that matters. Cut the warm-up. Whatever the page is for — booking, buying, understanding — put it in the first line and let the atmosphere follow.

## One idea per block

A paragraph that carries three ideas gets skimmed and none of them land. Splitting it into three short blocks costs nothing in reading time and roughly triples the chance that one of them is noticed.

## Write the buttons carefully

Labels are read far more often than body copy, and they're usually written last. "Get started" tells a visitor nothing about what happens next. "Book a 20-minute call" tells them the commitment, the format and the length in four words.

## Read it out loud

If a sentence can't be said in one breath, it won't be read in one glance. It's a crude test and it catches most of the problems.

Good web writing isn't shorter for its own sake. It's arranged so that someone who reads a quarter of it still leaves with the point.
