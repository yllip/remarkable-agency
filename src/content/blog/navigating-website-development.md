Handover is the riskiest moment in a website project. A design is approved as a set of pictures, and then someone has to decide what happens at every size, state and edge case the pictures didn't cover. Left to the end, those decisions get made under deadline pressure by whoever is closest to the keyboard.

## Build the system before the pages

We start with the tokens — spacing, type scale, colour, radius — and the handful of components that every page is assembled from. Once those exist, a new page is a composition rather than a construction, and it inherits every decision already made.

## Name things once

A component named the same way in the design file, the codebase and the CMS removes an entire category of miscommunication. It sounds trivial. It's the single change that most reliably speeds up the second half of a project.

## Keep the feedback loop short

Static previews of real pages, on real content, from the first week. Reviewing a running site catches problems that no amount of staring at artboards will: the wrap that ruins a heading, the image that loads late, the tap target that's a little too small.

None of this is exotic. It's mostly a matter of deciding things in the right order, and writing them down where the next person will look.
