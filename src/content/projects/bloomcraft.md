BloomCraft came to us as a wholesaler. Florists across three counties bought
from them, event companies specified them, and almost nobody outside the trade
had heard the name. The work they were proudest of — installations for galleries
and fashion houses — lived on a phone camera roll.

## Selling the eye, not the stem

We stopped describing BloomCraft as a supplier. The new positioning leads with
authorship: these are people who compose with flowers, and the wholesale
business is the proof they can do it at scale. Everything downstream followed
from that one decision.

The identity is deliberately spare — a single weight, generous margins, no
ornament — because the work is already loud. Photography carries the brand, and
the system exists to stay out of its way.

## A site that behaves like a portfolio

The old site was a catalogue with a contact form at the end. The new one opens
with a single installation, full bleed, and lets you move through the work
before it asks you for anything. Trade ordering still exists, one level down,
where the buyers who need it already know to look.

Six months after launch, direct commissions had gone from a fifth of revenue to
just under half, and the wholesale accounts stayed.
