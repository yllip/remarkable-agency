Mystic Haven had a booking problem that looked like a photography problem. The
images were beautiful and interchangeable — the same terrace, the same
sunset, the same copy about escape that every property in the region was
running.

## The town is the product

Guests were not coming for the rooms. They were coming for a place where nothing
is scheduled and the afternoons are long. So the brand moved outward: the
streets, the market, the walk back up the hill. The property became the vantage
point rather than the subject.

## Fewer rooms, better sold

We cut the site from forty pages to eleven and let each room type carry its own
argument, with rates in plain view. Removing the urgency devices — the counters,
the last-room warnings — turned out to raise conversion rather than lower it.

Direct bookings rose 38% in the first season, and the average stay lengthened by
a night.
