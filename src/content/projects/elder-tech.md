Every competitor in the category was selling to the same person: an adult child,
worried, buying for a parent who had not been consulted. The products looked it
— beige, oversized, apologetic. Elder Tech had better hardware and the same
apologetic brand.

## Talk to the person using it

We moved the audience by one generation. The brand now addresses the person who
will actually live with the device, and it assumes competence: plain language,
no diminutives, no medical blue. The reassurance the family needs is still
there, but it sits in the detail — warranty, support, setup — rather than in the
tone of voice.

## Designing for hands and eyes that have earned some respect

The interface work started from the constraints and refused to make them
cosmetic. Type scales up without the layout collapsing, contrast holds at every
size, and every control is reachable one-handed. The result reads as considered
rather than clinical, which is the whole argument the brand is making.

Support calls in the first month of ownership fell by a third, and the product
began appearing in reviews that had never covered the category before.
